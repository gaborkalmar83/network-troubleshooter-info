How to choose the best WiFi channel

A crowded WiFi channel can make a good router feel slow. This guide explains what a WiFi channel is, why channels 1, 6 and 11 get so much attention on 2.4 GHz, how 5 GHz and 6 GHz differ, and how to find the least crowded channel with an Android phone.

What a WiFi channel is

A WiFi router talks on a slice of radio spectrum called a channel. Every router and access point near you picks one. When two networks use the same channel, or channels that overlap, their devices have to take turns, and speed and stability can drop. On 2.4 GHz other things add noise as well, such as microwave ovens and Bluetooth devices.

Why 1, 6 and 11 on 2.4 GHz

The 2.4 GHz band is divided into channels spaced 5 MHz apart, but each WiFi signal is about 20 MHz wide. That is why neighbouring channel numbers overlap heavily. Channels 1, 6 and 11 are far enough apart that they do not overlap each other in most regions, so they are the usual choice. Some regions also allow channels up to 13.

Sharing exactly the same channel with a neighbour is usually less harmful than partly overlapping them. Devices on the same channel can coordinate and take turns, while partly overlapping signals just look like noise to each other. So pick the one of 1, 6 and 11 that the fewest and weakest networks near you are using. On 2.4 GHz a 20 MHz channel width is normally the safe choice, because wider channels take up even more room.

5 GHz and 6 GHz

The 5 GHz band has many more channels, and at the standard 20 MHz width they do not overlap each other, so there is much more room. Wider channels (40, 80 or 160 MHz) are faster but combine several 20 MHz channels, so fewer separate choices remain. Some 5 GHz channels share spectrum with radar, and a router may have to leave them when it detects it. That feature is called DFS and can cause a short interruption.

6 GHz, used by WiFi 6E and newer, adds even more room, but you need a router and a phone that both support it. Higher bands generally carry less far and pass through walls less well than 2.4 GHz, so a perfect channel does not help much where the signal is weak.

Find the least crowded channel with an Android phone

  1. Open the WiFi screen of Ultimate Network Troubleshooter. The app scans nearby networks on 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz and, where your phone supports it, 6 GHz.
  2. Read the channel advice. The app rates each channel by how much the networks around you overlap it, weighted by how strong they are, and says in plain words which channel is least crowded in your area.
  3. Note which channel and band your own router uses. The home screen shows the channel of the network you are connected to.
  4. If a clearly better channel exists, change it in your router's own settings. Menu names differ between routers, so check the manual of yours. On 2.4 GHz pick 1, 6 or 11.
  5. Scan again and run a speed test to compare. Android only allows a few WiFi scans in a couple of minutes, so give the app a moment between scans.

Android requires precise location permission before any app may list nearby WiFi networks. The app uses it on your phone only.

Do it for the whole house

Your router may look fine in the living room and crowded upstairs. Walk mode repeats the measurement as you move through your home and counts every neighbour at the loudest level it was heard on the walk, so the suggested channel suits the whole house. The WiFi heat map guide shows how.

What channel advice cannot do

Many routers already pick a decent channel on their own, and a better channel does not fix a weak signal, a slow internet plan or a faulty cable. In a block of flats with dozens of networks, any channel will be shared. Channel advice is a snapshot: neighbours change their settings, so check again after you notice slowdowns.

Frequently asked questions

Is channel 6 always the best on 2.4 GHz?

No. Channels 1, 6 and 11 are the three that do not overlap each other in most regions, but the best one is the one that the fewest and weakest networks near you are using.

Should I use 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz?

5 GHz usually gives more speed and more room when your device is in the same room as the router or one wall away. 2.4 GHz reaches further and through walls better but is more crowded.

Does the app change my router's channel?

No. The app only measures and advises. You change the channel in your router's own settings.
