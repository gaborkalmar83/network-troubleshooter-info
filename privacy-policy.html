Ultimate Network Troubleshooter

Privacy policy. Last updated: 26 November 2026.

Ultimate Network Troubleshooter is an Android app that analyses Wi-Fi and mobile networks and offers network tools. It is made by Kalmarium Services B.V., the Netherlands ("we"). There is no account, no sign-up and we run no analytics of our own. We run no server that receives your data. The free version shows Google AdMob ads, which may collect data under Google's consent rules (see Ads below).

What stays on your phone

Everything the app measures or discovers stays on your device: Wi-Fi networks, signal strength, the devices found on your network, port scan results, the network map, reports, Bluetooth scan results, and your settings. Reports and maps leave the phone only when you choose Share or Save, and then go where you send them.

Location and nearby devices

Android requires precise location access before an app may list nearby Wi-Fi networks, mobile antennas and Bluetooth devices. The app uses it on your phone to show those and to place you on the antenna map. Bluetooth scanning needs the Nearby devices permission. Neither is used for tracking. Bluetooth results never leave the phone. Your position is sent off the phone only to the antenna map services listed below.

App activity (optional)

The App activity tool shows which apps use the network and how much. It reads Android usage statistics on your phone after you grant Usage access. The numbers are kept in memory until you close the report, nothing is sent to us or any other server, and you can revoke Usage access at any time in Android settings.

Services the app contacts, only when you use the related feature

Antenna map: from version 1.1.2 the antenna lookup runs only when you open the Cellular screen or tap Scan again on that screen. It does not run in the background, on other screens or after you leave the screen, and if you open the screen again within 10 minutes at the same cell and place, the app shows the earlier result and sends nothing. Earlier versions looked up while the app was open and location access was granted. When the lookup runs, the app sends the identity of the mobile cell your phone is connected to (mobile country and network code, area code, cell id and radio type) to BeaconDB and, when the app build includes an OpenCelliD access key, to OpenCelliD. It does not send nearby Wi-Fi network identifiers. To draw the masts around you it also sends your current position (to five decimal places) to OpenStreetMap Overpass servers, which are asked for masts within 3 km, and, when the build includes an OpenCelliD key, a box of about 1 km around your position to OpenCelliD. The map view loads map tiles from the OpenStreetMap tile servers, which receive the map area you look at. All of these services also see your internet address.

Speed test: uses Cloudflare's public speed test servers (speed.cloudflare.com). iPerf3: connects to the server you pick.

IP and provider, Whois, Exposure check: your public IP address or the domain you enter is sent to RIPEstat, the internet registries (via IANA) and Shodan InternetDB to look up its owner and open ports.

Cloud and services: connects to about 44 public websites and reads the public status pages of Cloudflare, Microsoft, Google, Amazon, GitHub, Slack, Atlassian and OpenAI.

Network tools (ping, route map, DNS, website check, port scan, device scan) send packets to the hosts or devices you choose, as the tool name says. Only scan networks you own or have permission to test.

Diagnostics and problem reports

From version 1.1.1, if the app crashes, the crash text (an error trace with a time stamp) is saved in the app's private storage on your phone, and the app keeps the last few hundred log lines of the current session in memory only. Settings > Diagnostics shows exactly what a problem report contains: the app version, your phone make, model and Android version, your language, the ad and purchase status, the last crash and the last log lines. The app never sends this report itself. Only if you tap Send report does your own e-mail app open with a draft addressed to kalmariumservices@gmail.com, which you can read, change or discard before sending; if no e-mail app is found, the report is copied to the clipboard instead. We run no crash-reporting service or diagnostics SDK of our own, and the app uploads no report itself. Google AdMob, which shows the ads in the free version, may collect diagnostics and an advertising ID under Google's consent rules (see Ads below).

Ads

The free version shows a banner ad from Google AdMob. To show and measure ads, Google and its partners may collect and process your advertising ID, IP address (which gives an approximate location), device information and ad interactions. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland the app asks for your consent before any ad is requested, and you can change your choice at any time in the app settings under Privacy choices. More about how Google uses data: policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

The one-time ad-free purchase removes all ads, and with them all ad-related data collection in the app.

Purchases

The ad-free purchase is handled by Google Play Billing. We receive no payment details. Google Play tells the app only whether your Google account owns the purchase.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access, correct or delete personal data and to object to processing. Because we hold no personal data about you ourselves, requests about advertising data are handled by Google; you can also reset or delete your advertising ID in your Android settings.

Website visits

This website counts page views with a cookie-free script that stores nothing on your device and sends only the page address, the referring website and the country (derived from your network address, which is not kept). It is switched off if your browser sends Do Not Track.
